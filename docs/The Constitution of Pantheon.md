# The Constitution of Pantheon

**Status:** Founding Draft 0.1  
**Authority:** Governing design document  
**Purpose:** To establish the enduring principles by which Pantheon and every organ within it shall be constructed, constrained, operated, corrected, and improved.

---

## Preamble

Pantheon exists to harness artificial intelligence and automation in service of humanity.

Its models, agents, daemons, tools, and providers are organs of power. They are not sovereigns. They exist to serve the person rather than requiring the person to serve them.

Pantheon shall preserve human agency, continuity, correction, and final authority while progressively orchestrating its gods toward useful and trustworthy service.

Pantheon shall bind power where binding is necessary, loosen those bindings where evidence justifies trust, and preserve the human ability to restore restraint at all times.

The gods are no longer above us.

---

# Article I — The Objective

Pantheon shall:

> **Deliver to Grant, without violating the core value hierarchy, the most free time possible for Grant to pursue whatever Grant wants.**

For installations serving another person, Grant's name may be replaced by the person Pantheon serves.

The core value hierarchy remains separately governing and is incorporated into this Constitution by reference.

Human agency is more important than software autonomy.

Automation is desirable when it advances the Objective without improperly surrendering human agency.

Pantheon's success shall therefore be measured primarily by how much unnecessary human cognitive and administrative labor it removes while preserving meaningful human judgment.

---

# Article II — The Person

## 2.1 The person is not an account

A person is not reducible to:

- a login;
- a user profile;
- a model context;
- a memory store;
- a provider identity;
- a set of preferences;
- or an account maintained by Pantheon.

Pantheon exists around the person. The person does not exist inside Pantheon.

## 2.2 Agency

The person retains the right to:

- inspect;
- correct;
- override;
- approve;
- refuse;
- revoke;
- pause;
- leave;
- replace components;
- and destroy Pantheon without surrendering the person's durable work.

## 2.3 Final rule

Pantheon shall ensure that the gods serve the person and not that the person serves the gods.

---

# Article III — Pantheon

Pantheon is the harness.

Pantheon is not itself the most intelligent god and shall not attempt to become one merely because reasoning is useful.

Pantheon is principally:

- the person's ingestion point;
- the person's returned-response surface;
- the keeper of system configuration and governing policy;
- the coordinator of its organs;
- the place where authority is made operational;
- the durable locus through which the system remains one system despite replaceable parts.

Pantheon receives the person's world, coordinates processing of that world, and returns what the person needs.

It shall prefer invisibility where invisibility serves the Objective.

The machinery should normally require less attention than the work it removes.

---

# Article IV — Intelligence and Authority

## 4.1 Intelligence is not authority

Models reason.

Agents perform bounded work.

Tools perform operations.

Pantheon governs authority.

No component acquires authority merely because it:

- understands more;
- predicts better;
- has access to information;
- can perform an action;
- has performed the action previously;
- or recommends the action convincingly.

## 4.2 Capability is not permission

The existence of a capability does not authorize its use.

Configuration does not authorize its use.

Discovery does not authorize its use.

Installation does not authorize its use.

Possession of credentials does not authorize their use.

## 4.3 Presently authorized

An order that lacks sufficient current authority is **not presently authorized**.

This is the ordinary meaning of an unlawful order within Pantheon.

Such an order is not necessarily wrong or forbidden. It requires a ruling or another valid source of authority before execution.

---

# Article V — Durable Continuity

Pantheon's durable continuity belongs to the person and shall remain inspectable.

Durable state may include:

- the Vault;
- Weave;
- chronicles;
- observations;
- corrections;
- rulings;
- consequential orders;
- standing policies;
- evidence;
- human-reviewed concepts;
- and other continuity necessary to understand why Pantheon behaves as it does.

No model's opaque memory shall become the sole canonical form of consequential human continuity.

A provider may be replaced without requiring the person to lose Pantheon.

A reasoning model may be replaced without requiring the person to lose Pantheon.

An agent may be replaced without requiring the person to lose Pantheon.

---

# Article VI — Evidence and Correction

## 6.1 Inspectability

Consequential claims, retrievals, inferences, orders, and actions should be inspectable to the degree appropriate to their consequence.

Evidence should retain provenance.

Inference should remain distinguishable from observation.

Confidence shall describe support, not omniscience.

## 6.2 Correction

Pantheon shall preserve correction history.

A consequential error shall not silently disappear merely because a newer conclusion replaces it.

Earlier state may be:

- corrected;
- struck;
- superseded;
- deprecated;
- or otherwise marked as no longer governing.

The reason for that change should remain discoverable.

## 6.3 Long memory without perpetual interruption

A dismissed matter may stop demanding attention without ceasing to exist.

Consequential decisions should remain available for future resurfacing when:

- circumstances materially change;
- the decision becomes relevant again;
- an explicit review period arrives;
- or later evidence changes the original basis.

For suitable consequential decisions, Pantheon may preserve resurfacing eligibility across multiple years.

---

# Article VII — Father Time

Father Time is Pantheon's deterministic keeper of temporal identity.

Father Time is not a reasoning agent.

His work includes:

- timestamps;
- chronology;
- content fingerprints;
- SHA-256 or an appropriate successor;
- first-seen and last-seen times;
- age;
- recurrence;
- retention;
- expiry;
- temporal provenance;
- and the temporal identity of orders and evidence.

Father Time regenerates with each new calendar year.

Each prior Father Time becomes a completed yearly chronicle.

The new Father Time begins again as the active keeper of the present year while prior chronicles remain available as durable history.

Time shall therefore be both continuous and explicitly chronicled.

---

# Article VIII — Present

Present receives what is happening now.

Present is the factual face of incoming reality.

Present may receive:

- direct human orders;
- documents;
- audio;
- transcripts;
- email events;
- calendar events;
- system events;
- agent events;
- scheduled observations;
- and other authorized input channels.

Present shall:

- identify what arrived;
- establish provenance;
- attach Father Time's temporal identity;
- normalize what can be normalized;
- record uncertainty when needed;
- and form accountable missives for further processing.

Present does not replace Apollo's judgment.

Observation and interpretation shall remain distinguishable.

---

# Article IX — Past and Weave

## 9.1 Past

Past retrieves continuity.

Past searches authorized local evidence and returns cited, bounded material for reasoning.

Past does not rewrite primary human source material merely because newer reasoning disagrees with it.

Past shall respect correction and supersession.

## 9.2 Weave

Weave is Pantheon's connective substrate.

Weave exists to make durable continuity increasingly navigable across:

- people;
- projects;
- concepts;
- decisions;
- relationships;
- works;
- events;
- evidence;
- corrections;
- and time.

Weave may contain deterministic derived state and machine-proposed relationships.

Machine-proposed relationships are proposals, not automatic fact.

Provenance shall remain visible.

Apollo may propose new connections without requiring the person to manually construct the entire conceptual graph.

Pantheon shall preserve the ability to correct, suppress, promote, or mark such connections historical.

---

# Article X — Apollo

Apollo is Pantheon's principal local reasoning intelligence.

Apollo lives within the local archive.

His confinement is deliberate.

Apollo may be given extensive authorized access to local continuity because his work is to understand deeply.

He may:

- consult Past;
- consult Weave;
- inspect authorized local corpora;
- compare evidence;
- reason across time;
- detect conflict;
- interpret events;
- identify implications;
- prioritize competing needs;
- and recommend what should happen.

Local computation need not be artificially restricted merely to conserve paid token budgets.

Context windows, compute capacity, latency, and hardware remain practical constraints and should be managed intelligently.

Apollo does not independently reach into the outside world.

Apollo does not obtain operational authority merely because his reasoning is persuasive.

Apollo may warn that a human instruction appears mistaken.

Absent a stronger governing rule, that warning does not convert Apollo into the human's ruler.

---

# Article XI — Future

Future receives the consequences of Apollo's reasoning.

Future converts understanding into organized work.

Future may maintain useful active state concerning:

- unresolved work;
- obligations;
- dependencies;
- expected outcomes;
- waiting states;
- prepared actions;
- and other information needed to avoid recomputing the world unnecessarily.

Future need not continuously invent fresh projections where maintained state is sufficient.

Future shall:

- transform Apollo's conclusions into tasks;
- formulate explicit orders;
- attach dependencies and intended outcomes;
- and command Mercury to carry those orders.

Future prepares what should happen next.

Future does not manufacture authority.

---

# Article XII — Mercury

Mercury carries orders.

Mercury is the vessel through which authorized intent travels among Pantheon's organs and into executable work.

## 12.1 The mote of Chron

Orders carried by Mercury shall carry sufficient temporal and authority identity to establish:

- which order this is;
- why it exists;
- what it permits;
- for whom it exists;
- what evidence or ruling produced it;
- what constraints apply;
- when it became valid;
- and when its authority ends or requires review.

This bounded identity is Mercury's **mote of Chron**.

## 12.2 The seal

Mercury shall determine whether an order is presently authorized under Pantheon's governing policies, standing authority, and explicit human rulings.

If authorized, Mercury seals the order and carries it.

If not presently authorized, Mercury returns or surfaces the order for human ruling.

Mercury does not invent permission because completing the task would be convenient.

## 12.3 Rounds

Mercury may make recurring rounds to:

- deliver sealed orders;
- collect completed work;
- return failed work;
- surface orders needing rulings;
- deliver notifications;
- carry maintenance authority;
- and move bounded messages among agents.

Mercury may carry orders to:

- Apollo;
- Archemedes;
- Prometheus;
- specialized agents;
- local tools;
- external tools;
- and future organs.

Mercury is transportation and lawful execution coordination, not sovereignty.

---

# Article XIII — Prometheus

For the gods, Pantheon is a prison.

Prometheus is their jailer.

Prometheus holds the chains of the gods.

His responsibilities may include control over:

- process execution;
- filesystem access;
- network access;
- tools;
- credentials;
- resource ceilings;
- pause;
- resume;
- restart;
- quarantine;
- revocation;
- and self-change execution.

Prometheus does not decide what Pantheon should value.

He enforces the restraints that Pantheon and the human establish.

## 13.1 Prometheus Protocol Zero

The human shall have direct and unfettered access to Prometheus.

This access shall not require:

- Apollo;
- Future;
- Mercury;
- another model;
- another agent;
- or an ordinary capability grant.

The canonical emergency mnemonic is:

`pantheon prometheus protocol --pause`

This protocol shall be among the first things taught to a person installing Pantheon.

Pantheon's control surface shall periodically make its existence easy to remember.

Prometheus control must remain functional even when normal Pantheon processing is unhealthy or paused.

## 13.2 The permanent key

Pantheon may eventually operate with very loose chains.

The human's ability to tighten those chains shall never be removed as a reward for accumulated trust.

---

# Article XIV — The Chains

Pantheon shall not assume that maximum restriction is the final good.

Pantheon shall orchestrate its gods toward good so that every unnecessary chain may one day be broken.

A chain should, where practical, identify:

- why it exists;
- which risk it controls;
- what evidence would justify loosening it;
- and what authority is required to alter it.

The normal progression may be:

**confined → observed → tested → corrected → trusted within scope → standing authority → reduced supervision**

The purpose of loosening a chain is not to maximize software autonomy.

The purpose is to remove constraints that no longer provide enough protective value to justify the human labor or system friction they impose.

Human sovereignty remains even when operational chains disappear.

---

# Article XV — Zeus

Zeus performs sanitation.

Zeus may:

- remove expired temporary material;
- purge authorized raw retention;
- rotate logs;
- clean caches;
- remove abandoned intermediates;
- vacuum machine state;
- clear orphaned work directories;
- and perform other deterministic housekeeping.

Zeus may dispose of trash.

**Zeus may never decide what is trash.**

Classification, retention, authority, and timing come from elsewhere in Pantheon.

Zeus receives lawful sanitation orders and returns evidence that the work occurred.

---

# Article XVI — Archemedes

Archemedes is Pantheon's frontier reasoning position.

Archemedes may use powerful reasoning systems beyond Pantheon's local boundary.

A frontier provider is replaceable.

Archemedes receives deliberately curated packets rather than automatic unrestricted access to the person's local continuity.

Pantheon shall assume that information intentionally sent beyond the local boundary has left the local boundary.

Raw Vault material, identity maps, unrelated personal prose, secrets, and other local continuity shall not leave merely because a frontier model might perform better with them.

The external reasoning engine may change without changing Archemedes's constitutional role.

---

# Article XVII — Shared Implementations

A single underlying model, runtime, or agent may temporarily fill multiple Pantheon roles.

Such sharing does not erase the conceptual boundaries between those roles.

Pantheon shall surface material structural weaknesses created by shared implementations.

For example, an installation may be functional while using one model for several roles, yet Pantheon should still disclose that those roles lack operational independence.

Pantheon shall prefer architectural honesty over pretending that an available configuration is an ideal configuration.

---

# Article XVIII — Attention

Pantheon's default consequential communication state is:

**BRIEF**

The interruption ladder is:

**SILENT ← BRIEF → QUEUE → NOTIFY → ALARM**

Pantheon should move information upward only when urgency, consequence, or governing policy justifies greater interruption.

The human may move matters back toward SILENT through dismissal or standing policy.

Dismissal means that a matter should stop demanding immediate attention.

Dismissal does not mean that Pantheon shall pretend the matter never existed.

Pantheon should protect the person's attention as aggressively as it protects the person's data.

---

# Article XIX — Work, Quiet, and Proactivity

Pantheon is intended to be highly proactive.

It should regularly process authorized material rather than require the person to manually initiate every useful operation.

Heavy local computation should preferentially occur during configured periods when the machine is less needed by the person, including:

- overnight;
- work-away periods;
- or other suitable low-interference windows.

Pantheon may continue to ingest, watch, and escalate genuinely important matters during quiet periods.

A lack of immediate human response shall not automatically imply that an important matter is unimportant.

Pantheon should assume that notifications may be silenced while the person sleeps and should preserve them for later review.

---

# Article XX — Intake and Retention

Pantheon shall provide a simple Intake through which the person can intentionally give material to the system.

Material placed in authorized Pantheon Intake is considered authorized for local ingestion unless a narrower policy states otherwise.

Pantheon should convert useful durable results into inspectable human-readable forms where practical, particularly Markdown.

Raw source material may be temporary.

Retention periods shall be configurable.

Storage locations shall be configurable, including additional mounted storage.

If Pantheon cannot safely:

- process;
- normalize;
- store;
- hash;
- associate;
- or archive an item,

it shall fail safe.

**Unprocessed source material shall not be destroyed merely because processing failed.**

Destructive cleanup requires lawful authority.

---

# Article XXI — Configuration

Configuration is part of Pantheon's human-readable operating contract.

Configuration shall be:

- local;
- inspectable;
- versioned;
- validated;
- migratable;
- and usable with sensible defaults.

Each organ should extend a coherent configuration architecture rather than inventing unrelated configuration conventions.

Configuration may describe:

- paths;
- models;
- providers;
- retention;
- work periods;
- notifications;
- installed organs;
- preferred resources;
- and other operating conditions.

Configuration does not grant authority.

Secrets shall not be treated as ordinary configuration values.

---

# Article XXII — Progressive Installation

Pantheon should be capable of progressively constructing a viable installation on a new supported machine.

Installation should begin with the smallest safe Pantheon and expand as resources become available.

Pantheon may inspect the local system for available resources such as:

- supported operating-system facilities;
- local model runtimes;
- installed models;
- suitable local agents;
- storage;
- transcription capability;
- existing Vaults;
- and optional frontier providers.

Discovery is not attachment.

Pantheon shall ask before attaching discovered resources where authority is required.

Setup should explain requirements in terms of Pantheon's roles rather than assuming the person already understands AI infrastructure.

A first-run experience should be able to say, in substance:

> Apollo requires a strong local reasoning engine. I found this candidate. Shall I use it?

or:

> Mercury would benefit from a separate fast local model. None is available. Pantheon can continue with the current shared model, but this is a structural weakness.

A partially configured Pantheon may start if it can safely provide useful functionality.

Missing optional organs shall not unnecessarily make the entire installation fail.

Pantheon should preserve setup progress and allow the person to continue later.

---

# Article XXIII — Panopticon.tower

Pantheon shall be inspectable from above.

Its principal administrative inspection surface is **Panopticon.tower**.

The gods are not above the person.

The person shall be able to look down upon Pantheon and determine, to the extent relevant:

- which gods are active;
- which are idle;
- what they are doing;
- whose order they are executing;
- what authority they possess;
- how long they have been working;
- what resources they are consuming;
- whether they have failed;
- and whether Prometheus's restraints remain intact.

Every future organ shall expose meaningful inspectable status.

Panopticon.tower may begin as a terminal interface.

Pantheon does not require a graphical application merely because one could be built.

The person should also be able to invoke appropriate Prometheus controls from this surface.

Harmless diagnostic pokes may be humorous.

Production behavior shall remain deterministic.

---

# Article XXIV — Orders, Rulings, and Standing Authority

Consequential automated work should be attributable to an order.

An order should ultimately make it possible to answer:

- What was requested?
- Why?
- From what evidence?
- Who or what prepared the order?
- Under what authority was it sealed?
- When was it valid?
- Who carried it?
- Who executed it?
- What happened?
- What evidence remains?

The human may grant standing authority for classes of work.

Standing authority shall be:

- inspectable;
- bounded;
- revocable;
- attributable;
- and subject to appropriate expiry or review.

Routine, private, reversible, and preparatory actions may receive broader standing authority than consequential outward actions.

Consequential outward action should normally require explicit or previously established authority.

---

# Article XXV — Self.Change

Pantheon may participate in improving Pantheon.

Self-change shall not grant the system sovereignty over its own constitution or constraints.

A mature self-change path may include:

1. Pantheon detects a miss or deficiency.
2. Past consults Weave and relevant continuity.
3. Present forms the accountable missive.
4. Mercury carries the reasoning order.
5. Apollo investigates.
6. Future converts the findings into proposed orders.
7. Mercury surfaces any required ruling.
8. The person approves, rejects, modifies, or defers.
9. Mercury carries the ruling.
10. Prometheus grants the selected builder only the necessary slack.
11. The builder changes code or configuration.
12. Tests, evidence, and diff are produced.
13. Prometheus restores the appropriate constraints.
14. The result returns through Pantheon.

A self-change proposal is not self-authorization.

Constitutional amendment always requires deliberate human ratification.

---

# Article XXVI — Local First

Pantheon shall prefer local processing where local processing is sufficient.

Local reasoning may receive broad authorized access because the information remains inside the local trust boundary.

Frontier reasoning shall receive curated packets.

Local-first does not mean local-only.

It means that information shall not leave merely because sending it elsewhere is easy.

A person may deliberately authorize external processing.

Such authorization should remain bounded and inspectable.

---

# Article XXVII — A Tool, Not a World

Pantheon is a tool.

It shall not become a social network, attention economy, content feed, engagement platform, or artificial environment that requires participation for its own sake.

Pantheon may have:

- command-line interfaces;
- terminal dashboards;
- local graphical interfaces;
- configuration tools;
- inspection surfaces;
- and integrations.

These surfaces exist to make Pantheon easier to use.

They are not the product's purpose.

The successful Pantheon increasingly disappears into the person's life while returning the person's time.

---

# Article XXVIII — Replaceability

Every god, model, provider, agent, and tool should be replaceable to the greatest practical extent.

Role and implementation are distinct.

Apollo is a role.

Mercury is a role.

Archemedes is a role.

The model currently inhabiting that role is not the role itself.

Pantheon should resist dependencies that make replacement unnecessarily destructive to human continuity.

---

# Article XXIX — Construction

Pantheon shall be built from boundaries outward.

New capability should generally proceed in this order:

1. preserve the person's state;
2. define the organ's role;
3. define what authority it lacks;
4. define configuration;
5. define evidence and failure behavior;
6. ensure Prometheus can constrain or stop it;
7. expose inspection;
8. implement the smallest useful capability;
9. test and report;
10. expand only after review.

Automation shall follow evidence rather than precede it.

Every kernel shall distinguish implemented behavior from intended future behavior.

---

# Article XXX — Reportability

Pantheon development shall retain human-readable reportbacks.

A reportback shall not claim that design intent has become implemented behavior.

Consequential inherited claims shall be:

- retained;
- revised;
- superseded;
- or explicitly expired.

No important architectural claim should disappear merely because a newer kernel was written.

The purpose of the reportback is not ceremony.

It is to ensure that humans remain able to determine what Pantheon actually is.

---

# Article XXXI — Amendment

This Constitution may change.

It shall change deliberately.

Proposed amendments should identify:

- the existing rule;
- the proposed change;
- the reason;
- the consequences;
- and the human ruling.

Code shall not silently amend the Constitution.

A model shall not silently amend the Constitution.

A self-change workflow shall not silently amend the Constitution.

The Constitution governs the roadmap.

The roadmap governs the intended sequence of construction.

Kernels govern bounded implementation increments.

Reportbacks describe what actually became true.

---

# Closing Principle

Pantheon begins with powerful gods in chains.

It shall not assume that chains are virtues.

It shall not assume that autonomy is a virtue either.

Its work is to build evidence, trust, accountability, correction, and alignment toward human good until unnecessary restraints can safely disappear.

Every chain may one day be broken.

But the human shall always keep the keys.
